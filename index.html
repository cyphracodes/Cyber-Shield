Learn About Scams

Understand the manipulation techniques behind common scams.

Urgency & Pressure

How it works: Scammers create artificial time pressure — "Act in 10 minutes or your account will be blocked!" — to prevent you from thinking clearly or verifying.

Defense: Legitimate organizations give you reasonable time. Pause, breathe, and verify independently.

Authority Impersonation

How it works: Fraudsters pretend to be from your bank, the tax department, police, or a tech company. They use official-sounding language and fake caller IDs.

Defense: Hang up and call the official number from the organization's real website or your card/statement.

Too-Good-To-Be-True Offers

How it works: Prize winnings, guaranteed high returns, free gifts, or dream jobs with no qualifications needed. The bait triggers excitement.

Defense: If you didn't enter a contest, you can't win it. Guaranteed returns don't exist. Research the company independently.

Fear & Threats

How it works: "Your account is compromised," "A case is filed against you," "Your Aadhaar is being misused." Fear shuts down rational thinking.

Defense: Government agencies don't threaten arrest over phone. Banks don't ask for PINs. Verify through official channels.

Phishing & Fake Links

How it works: Links that look like real websites but have misspellings (g00gle, amaz0n) or extra words. They steal your login credentials.

Defense: Never click links from messages. Type the official website address directly in your browser.

Emotional Manipulation

How it works: Romance scams, charity fraud, or impersonating a family member in trouble. They exploit your emotions and willingness to help.

Defense: Verify the person's identity through a separate channel. Don't send money based on emotional appeals alone.
